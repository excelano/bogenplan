Bogenplan (iPhone) — Privacy

Bogenplan shows the calendars on your iPhone laid out by the Sun's position, so it works with two personal things: your calendar events and your location. This section says what it does with each. In brief: it reads both on your iPhone, keeps nothing of your calendars, keeps your approximate coordinates on the device, and sends nothing anywhere. Bogenplan has no server, no account and no network code at all. Its App Store privacy label reads “Data Not Collected”.

Your calendars

With your permission, Bogenplan reads the events in the calendars on your iPhone through Apple's calendar framework, EventKit, to draw them on the day you are looking at. It reads them only while it is on screen and does not copy them into storage of its own. It creates an event only when you set a moment, a zero-length event with an alert, in your default calendar for new events, and it never changes or deletes an event. The calendars it reads are the ones your iPhone already has, including any accounts you have added in Settings; Bogenplan does not connect to those accounts itself.

Your position

With your permission, Bogenplan reads your approximate location, not your precise one, each time it comes on screen, to find solar noon, sunrise and sunset where you are. Location is not used in the background. If you withdraw the permission, Bogenplan forgets the coordinates it had.

What Bogenplan saves

The app's preferences hold the coordinates in use, the calendars you have chosen to hide (by their identifiers, not their contents), your choice of spans or kiloseconds, the precision, whether clock times are shown, and the zoom of the day view. Nothing else is written. Removing the app removes all of it.

Nothing is sent

Bogenplan contains no code that opens a network connection: no analytics, no crash reporter, no update check and no third-party libraries beyond Excelano's own open-source Bogenzeit engine, which runs on the device. Apple, separately, may show developers anonymous install counts and, from people who have turned on Share With App Developers, crash reports. That setting belongs to Apple and covers every app; it is on the iPhone under Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

Checking this

Bogenplan's source is public at github.com/excelano/bogenplan. Calendars are read in CalendarStore.swift and location comes through SolarPlace.swift. This text lives in the same repository, at packaging/privacy-entry.html, and its history there is the record of every change.
